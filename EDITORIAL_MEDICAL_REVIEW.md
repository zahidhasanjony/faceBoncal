# Health-information review before publishing

This is an editorial checklist, not a change to the supplied SRT or MP3.

- **Vitamin D is not literally the only key.** It helps intestinal calcium absorption and bone health. The line that without it *no calcium can enter bone* is too absolute.
- **Kidney/vascular calcification is not inevitable.** The voiceover says calcium will instead form stones in the kidney or blood vessels; this should not be presented as an automatic result of vitamin D deficiency. Excess calcium supplementation can increase kidney stone risk in some settings, but the causes and risks vary.
- **Routine K2 supplementation is not established for everyone.** The video can discuss vitamin K from food, but telling every viewer to ensure D3 and K2 supplements is stronger than the evidence supports. Do not self-medicate without clinical need.
- **Bone remodeling changes gradually.** The age-30 sleeping-bricklayer metaphor is a simplified story; aging is not a sudden biological switch at 30.
- **Bone density, pain and exercise:** knee/back pain alone cannot diagnose osteoporosis. Weight-bearing and strengthening activity supports bone health, but established osteoporosis may need diagnosis and additional treatment; "reverse bone loss" is not guaranteed.
- **Twenty minutes of brisk walking** can be a useful starting point, but exercise advice should account for age, fragility fractures and physical limitations.

Suggested editorial sign-off: check claims against NIH Office of Dietary Supplements (Vitamin D / Calcium Fact Sheets) and osteoporosis guidance from a clinician before releasing a health advice video. If rewriting narration is approved, regenerate the MP3 and SRT together to avoid timing mismatches.
